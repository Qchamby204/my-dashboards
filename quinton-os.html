01

Strategic Thinking

Learner

You are drawn to the process of understanding.

Learning itself is rewarding to you. Your personalized report describes depth rather than skimming, demanding subjects, and curiosity about other people’s strengths, interests and goals.

When it helps

You can stay engaged with difficult material, keep developing your skills and get to know people through thoughtful questions.

When it may hinder

Learning can continue beyond the point where it serves the task. You may also expect others to value learning as much as you do.

A question to sit with

What is this curiosity serving right now: understanding, useful action, or enjoyment? Have I made room for the kind of learning I actually need?

AmplifyGive your curiosity a worthwhile question or a person to understand more fully.

RefineFor applied learning, notice when you have enough to take the next step. Learning for pleasure does not need to become a deliverable.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] pp. 3–4; [5] pp. 3–5.

02

Influencing

Significance

You want your contribution to matter—and to be recognized.

This theme involves impact, influence, independence and being valued for your contribution. Your report also describes encouraging capable people and preferring room to determine how your work gets done.

When it helps

You can bring ambition to meaningful work and help others recognize the value of their own contribution.

When it may hinder

Concern for recognition may be read as concern for status. Appearing too controlled or concealing vulnerability can make it harder for others to know how to support you.

A question to sit with

Whose respect matters to me here—and what contribution do I want that respect to recognize? Have I made it clear what support I need?

AmplifyChoose contributions whose importance you can explain beyond the attention they receive.

RefineLet appreciation matter without making it the only test of value. Make a need for feedback or help visible.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] pp. 5–6; [5] pp. 6–7.

03

Executing

Achiever

You find satisfaction in making tangible progress.

Your report emphasizes effort, stamina, precision and results. It also describes a tension between devotion to work and wanting time for family, friends, solitude and recreation.

When it helps

You can sustain effort, finish demanding work and help others see why their contribution matters.

When it may hinder

The drive to finish can become overcommitment or frustration with a different pace. Work may absorb attention that you also want to give to the rest of your life.

A question to sit with

What is enough for this task? What important part of my life becomes less visible when I judge the day mainly by what I completed?

AmplifyDirect your effort toward the commitments that genuinely deserve it, including those outside work.

RefineNotice a completed result before raising the standard. Check capacity before agreeing to more, and allow breaks.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] pp. 7–8; [5] pp. 8–10.

04

Influencing

Self-Assurance

You tend to trust your ability and your judgment.

The report describes an internal sense of direction and confidence in making choices. Your personalized insights also emphasize breaking complex ideas into understandable parts and explaining them plainly.

When it helps

You can offer direction when others are uncertain, explain a complicated choice and help a group move beyond hesitation.

When it may hinder

Confidence can reduce the impulse to consult others. The certainty of your delivery may make people hesitant to question you, whether or not your understanding is complete.

A question to sit with

What supports my confidence in this particular situation? Have I heard an informed view that could genuinely change my mind?

AmplifyOffer a clear, reasoned direction when it is useful; make the uncertainty clear too.

RefineInvite another view before settling the choice. Revisit what happened so confidence remains open to learning.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] pp. 9–10; [5] pp. 11–13.

05

Influencing

Competition

Comparison gives you a clear standard to pursue.

You are attentive to how performance compares with others. Your personalized report also describes using reason and often performing well at a steady pace, rather than being pressured to move faster.

When it helps

Relevant benchmarks can sharpen effort and encourage higher standards for you and the people working with you.

When it may hinder

Comparison can crowd out satisfaction or strain relationships. Pursuing the win can become a problem when it displaces principles or people.

A question to sit with

Am I using this comparison to improve, or allowing it to decide what matters? Would I still value the result without finishing ahead?

AmplifyChoose a benchmark that represents the kind of work you genuinely respect.

RefineKeep the standard useful without turning every interaction into a contest. Notice the effect on relationships.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] pp. 11–12; [5] pp. 14–15.

06

Strategic Thinking

Intellection

Thinking things through is valuable in its own right.

This theme describes reflection, introspection and enjoyment of substantial discussion. The report suggests that time to think and write can help clarify what you understand.

When it helps

You can bring considered ideas to the early stages of a project and make a complicated issue clearer through reflection.

When it may hinder

Silence or solitude can be mistaken for disengagement. A discussion may become more complicated than it needs to be while others are looking for a usable answer.

A question to sit with

Is more thought changing my understanding, or making it harder to communicate? Does the other person know why I have gone quiet?

AmplifyProtect some space to think and put an important idea into your own words.

RefineShare the useful conclusion first when the moment calls for it. Explain when you need thinking time.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] p. 13.

07

Executing

Arranger

You see how people and resources could work together.

Arranger combines organization with flexibility. You can reconsider the arrangement of people, tasks or resources while still pursuing the same objective.

When it helps

You can coordinate moving parts and find a more effective way to deliver a shared result.

When it may hinder

Continual rearrangement can confuse others. Taking ownership without asking can crowd out their responsibility or make them feel that control has been taken away.

A question to sit with

Is this change making the work better for everyone involved—or simply arranging it my way? Who needs to understand or own the change?

AmplifyUse your flexibility to resolve a real coordination problem.

RefineExplain what is changing and why. Ask before taking over ownership that belongs to someone else.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] p. 14.

08

Executing

Focus

A clear direction helps you decide what deserves attention.

Focus describes prioritizing, following through and making corrections to stay on course. Your report emphasizes goals, timelines and protection from distractions.

When it helps

You can make progress on what matters instead of repeatedly being pulled toward the next interruption.

When it may hinder

Concentration can outlast the usefulness of the original plan. You may miss changed priorities or be slow to notice another person’s immediate needs.

A question to sit with

Is this still the right priority? What might I be failing to notice because I am concentrating so well?

AmplifyGive an important objective a clear direction and some protected attention.

RefineLook up often enough to notice changed circumstances. Distinguish a distraction from an important person’s need.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] p. 15.

09

Strategic Thinking

Analytical

You want to understand the reasons behind an answer.

You look for causes, patterns and evidence. The report describes a reasoned approach that can help clarify what is happening and whether a plan is feasible.

When it helps

You can test assumptions, find useful explanations and bring evidence to an important choice.

When it may hinder

Questions can be experienced as criticism or distrust. Other people’s emotional, personal or subjective perspectives may not receive the space the report encourages you to give them.

A question to sit with

Am I helping us understand the idea, or making someone feel they must defend themselves? What perspective is not captured by the facts alone?

AmplifyBring credible evidence and useful questions to the point where they can improve the decision.

RefineExplain what your questions are trying to clarify. Acknowledge the other person’s perspective before asking the next one.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] p. 16.

10

Influencing

Command

You are comfortable bringing direction to a difficult moment.

Command describes presence, directness and readiness to take charge. The report emphasizes speaking up when others hesitate and helping people make commitments.

When it helps

You can address a difficult issue, remove a bottleneck and offer clarity when a group needs direction.

When it may hinder

Decisiveness can feel intimidating. Speaking with authority can lead to having the final word before others have had a real opportunity to contribute.

A question to sit with

Does this moment need my direction, or more room for someone else’s thinking? What happens in the conversation immediately after I speak?

AmplifyUse directness to name the issue and make the next step clear.

RefineLet others contribute before announcing your view. Notice tone and timing as well as the substance of your message.

Questions and directions above are proposed reflections, not conclusions about your behaviour.

Report basis: [34] p. 17.

03 / How your strengths combine

Two things can be true at once.

The Top 5 report describes how your themes interact. These pairings offer a way to understand mixed motivations without treating them as contradictions to eliminate.

Learner + Achiever

I want to understand it.
I also want to finish it.

The report pairs an appetite for understanding something new with a strong wish to complete a task. Both can serve you; the moment may ask for more of one than the other.

Consider: Does this situation need deeper understanding, or is it time to use what I already know?

Pairing: [5] pp. 5, 10. Question: suggested reflection.

Significance + Self-Assurance

I trust my judgment.
Other people’s views still matter.

The report describes self-evaluation and other people’s evaluations both shaping how you feel about yourself. Inner confidence does not mean appreciation or recognition stops being important.

Consider: Am I looking for information, encouragement or approval? Which of those do I actually need here?

Pairing: [5] pp. 7, 13. Question: suggested reflection.

Achiever + Competition

I made progress.
How does it compare?

One drive comes from completing things; another comes from comparing performance. A result can satisfy the first without satisfying the second.

Consider: Can I recognize the value of this result even when someone else did better? What is the comparison teaching me?

Pairing: [5] pp. 10, 15. Question: suggested reflection.

Learner + Significance

Understanding matters.
Sharing it can matter too.

The report suggests that an opportunity to demonstrate learning to an audience can be motivating. Learning and being recognized for a contribution may reinforce one another.

Consider: What do I understand well enough to make useful to someone else? Am I aiming for their understanding or their impression of me?

Pairing: [5] pp. 5, 7. Questions: suggested reflections.

Self-Assurance + Competition

I want to influence the result.
Not everything is mine to control.

The report describes a preference for situations where success is largely determined by factors you can control. That preference is worth noticing when important outcomes also depend on other people or circumstances.

Consider: What can I influence here? What would it look like to do my part well without taking responsibility for the entire outcome?

Pairing: [5] pp. 13, 15. Questions: suggested reflections.

A whole-profile reflection

My intention can be useful.
My delivery can still need work.

Self-Assurance, Analytical and Command each have report cautions about how confidence, questioning or directness may land. Considering them together is an interpretation—not a separately measured finding.

Consider: When I am trying to clarify, decide or help, does the other person experience room to think—or pressure to agree?

Underlying cautions: [34] pp. 10, 16–17. Combination and question: interpretation.

The question is not “Which part of me is wrong?”

It is “What is this situation asking of me, and how could I express the useful part of this strength more deliberately?”

04 / Keep the ranking in perspective

A lower theme is not a missing quality.

Gallup says the assessment does not mathematically quantify weaknesses. A weakness is identified by what interferes with success—not simply by a number near the bottom.

Relationship Building is less represented among your highest-ranked themes. That does not establish that you cannot build close relationships, care about others or respond well to them. Gallup explicitly notes that people can use strengths from other domains to reach the same outcomes.

Likewise, low Deliberative does not prove poor judgment, and low Adaptability does not prove rigidity. Those conclusions would go beyond the reports. Your actual choices, circumstances and feedback matter.

Report basis: [34] pp. 18–21. The cautions against specific labels are interpretations of those limits.

05 / A moment to reflect

Start with a real moment, not a label.

No fields to complete. No score to improve. Think about one experience—or use your own notebook when writing would help.

What happened the last time this showed up?

Recall a conversation, a decision or a piece of work. Stay with what actually happened before deciding what it says about you.

  1. Where did this tendency help?

    What did it allow me to understand, contribute, complete or clarify? Where could that contribution be useful again?

  2. Where did it create friction?

    Was it the strength itself, its intensity, the timing, or what the situation needed? What evidence or feedback supports that interpretation?

  3. What should I preserve—and what could I adjust?

    Keep the useful ability. Consider one change in how you express it: a question before a conclusion, a clearer finish line, an explanation before reorganizing, or a request for help.

  4. What might show me that the adjustment helped?

    A clearer conversation, better information, less unnecessary rework or more room for someone important. Later, notice what happened rather than assuming the idea worked.

For example: keep the analytical question; change how you introduce it.

Instead of treating Analytical as a flaw, consider whether explaining your purpose and acknowledging the other person’s view would let the same careful thinking become more useful. That is a possible adjustment, not a conclusion that your questioning is a problem.
