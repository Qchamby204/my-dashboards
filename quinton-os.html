Your top 10: amplify and refine

01 · Strategic Thinking

Learner

The process of understanding and improving gives you energy.

What the report highlights. Your personalized report emphasizes depth, demanding learning, curiosity about people and opportunities to develop your skills.

The contribution. You bring curiosity, growing capability and a willingness to work through unfamiliar material.

Possible overuse. Learning can displace the result you need, or you may expect others to value learning as much as you do.

Amplify

Choose a worthwhile question, explore it deeply and explain one useful idea to someone who can use it.

Refine

For applied learning, define what you need to understand before you begin. Stop or reassess when that question is answered. Keep curiosity-led learning separate and legitimate.

Ask yourself: Am I learning for enjoyment, or to support a specific result? Either is valid; which is this?

34 report, pp. 3–4; Top 5 report, pp. 3–5. Practice wording is a proposed application.

02 · Influencing

Significance

Meaningful contribution, influence and recognition matter to you.

What the report highlights. Your personalized report describes encouraging others to excel, wanting influence and preferring autonomy over how you work.

The contribution. You bring ambition to important work and can help others recognize what they are capable of contributing.

Possible overuse. Concern for reputation may eclipse contribution; appearing overly controlled may hide the support you need.

Amplify

Choose a contribution that matters and name who benefits. Invite encouragement from people whose judgment you value.

Refine

Check whether recognition is guiding the goal or replacing it. Make a concrete request for support when it would help.

Ask yourself: What contribution matters here, and whose feedback is actually useful?

34 report, pp. 5–6; Top 5 report, pp. 6–7. Practice wording is a proposed application.

03 · Executing

Achiever

Tangible progress and completing work are deeply motivating.

What the report highlights. Your personalized report highlights precision, independent work, measurable results and tension between work and other priorities.

The contribution. You bring sustained effort, attention to completion and energy that can encourage others to contribute.

Possible overuse. You may overcommit, expect others to match your pace or move straight to the next task without making room for rest and other commitments.

Amplify

Direct your effort toward a meaningful priority and make the intended result clear.

Refine

Define enough before beginning. Check capacity before accepting work, acknowledge completion and protect commitments beyond work.

Ask yourself: What would count as complete—and what must not be displaced to achieve it?

34 report, pp. 7–8; Top 5 report, pp. 8–10. Practice wording is a proposed application.

04 · Influencing

Self-Assurance

You trust your capabilities and your judgment.

What the report highlights. Your personalized report also describes taking complex subjects apart, explaining them plainly and making practical decisions about ambitious ideas.

The contribution. You can provide clear direction when uncertainty leaves others hesitant.

Possible overuse. Confidence may reduce consultation, while a certain tone can make others reluctant to challenge you.

Amplify

Offer a clear, plain-language view when others are stuck, while naming what you know and what remains uncertain.

Refine

Ask for a relevant different view before announcing yours. Review decisions and outcomes rather than treating conviction as proof.

Ask yourself: What evidence or perspective could change my current view?

34 report, pp. 9–10; Top 5 report, pp. 11–13. Practice wording is a proposed application.

05 · Influencing

Competition

Comparison with strong performance gives you a benchmark.

What the report highlights. Your personalized report describes testing your abilities, using reason and often performing well when able to work at your own steady pace.

The contribution. You can make standards concrete and motivate yourself and others to improve.

Possible overuse. Comparison can strain relationships or lead you to prioritize winning over principles.

Amplify

Choose a relevant benchmark that helps you improve something you genuinely value.

Refine

Check what the comparison is serving. Keep principles and relationships outside the cost of winning, and ask for feedback on the effect of your approach.

Ask yourself: Is this comparison improving my work, or pulling me away from what matters?

34 report, pp. 11–12; Top 5 report, pp. 14–15. Practice wording is a proposed application.

06 · Strategic Thinking

Intellection

Reflection and substantive thinking help you make sense of things.

The contribution. You can clarify ideas by thinking carefully, writing and discussing them in depth.

Possible overuse. Others may mistake quiet reflection for disengagement, or find the discussion more complex than the situation needs.

Amplify

Protect time to think or write before an important discussion. Turn reflection into a clearer explanation.

Refine

Tell people when you are thinking. Match the depth to the situation and give the simple version before adding detail.

Ask yourself: What level of depth will help this person or decision right now?

34 report, p. 13. Practice wording is a proposed application.

07 · Executing

Arranger

You look for effective ways to coordinate people and resources.

The contribution. You bring organization with flexibility, adjusting the moving parts to improve delivery.

Possible overuse. Continual rearrangement can confuse others; taking control without agreement can reduce their ownership.

Amplify

Bring your coordination skills to a project with moving parts and help clarify how they fit together.

Refine

Name the problem before reorganizing. Explain changes and agree on ownership rather than quietly taking over.

Ask yourself: What specific friction would this change solve—and who needs to understand it?

34 report, p. 14. Practice wording is a proposed application.

08 · Executing

Focus

Clear priorities help you direct effort and follow through.

The contribution. You can keep attention on an objective, avoid distractions and correct course.

Possible overuse. Strong concentration can obscure changed priorities or make you slow to respond to other people’s needs.

Amplify

Give an important goal a clear next step and a protected period of attention.

Refine

Pause at a useful checkpoint to ask whether the objective or someone’s immediate needs have changed.

Ask yourself: Is this still the right objective, and what needs attention now?

34 report, p. 15. Practice wording is a proposed application.

09 · Strategic Thinking

Analytical

You look for reasons, causes, evidence and relevant factors.

The contribution. You can clarify a situation by investigating assumptions and identifying patterns.

Possible overuse. Questions may sound like distrust or criticism; a fact-based approach can overlook the value of others’ personal perspectives.

Amplify

Join early enough to examine assumptions and use credible evidence to improve the plan.

Refine

Explain why you are asking, acknowledge the answer and include others’ perspectives. Act when the decision has enough evidence, even if it is not complete.

Ask yourself: Am I making the reasoning clearer, and have I understood the person’s perspective?

34 report, p. 16. Practice wording is a proposed application.

10 · Influencing

Command

You are comfortable taking charge and speaking directly.

The contribution. You can remove uncertainty, make commitments clear and provide direction in a difficult moment.

Possible overuse. Directness can feel intimidating, or you may give your view before others have had room to contribute.

Amplify

Use a clear, firm voice when direction or a commitment is genuinely needed.

Refine

Hear others first when their input matters. Adjust words and tone so clarity does not become pressure.

Ask yourself: Does this moment need direction, or do I need to make space for another view?

34 report, p. 17. Practice wording is a proposed application.
